The Biggest Units Are Selling at the Highest PSF

Did you know that in today's market, the biggest units are sometimes selling at the highest PSF? That wasn't how property used to work. Traditionally, the bigger the unit, the lower the PSF buyers paid.

Why is this happening?

Because family-sized homes have become scarce. Developers are building fewer large units, while demand from upgraders and multi-generational families remains strong. This means that as compared to last time, larger homes are now more expensive.

If you are planning to upgrade to a larger home, new ones might not be it. The premium for space may continue to widen over time.

Rethinking the CCR Premium

The tabooCCR is expensive.
The tabooSome come with weird layouts.
The tabooPrices don't move as much because of investor nature.

For many years, we all ruled out CCR because the gap between OCR and CCR has always been very wide. On regional averages, it still is.

But what if there are options in the Central Region at $2,500 psf, while the Outside Central Region has already done $3,300 psf?

Those are entry and peak figures, not averages — and that is exactly the point. A regional average hides the overlap at the edges: the best-priced CCR projects now open below what the strongest OCR launches are already achieving.

Strategic Upgrade Within the Same District (D15)

In 2016, I helped a client purchase a 2-bedroom unit in District 15. As his family plans evolved and the need to upgrade became clearer, we identified the right next move — one that aligned with his budget without over-leveraging, and prioritised proximity to good schools.

We timed the sale decisively and sold it without viewing, achieving $300,000 in profit over 3 years. The proceeds were reinvested into a 3-bedroom + study unit within the same district, secured at an attractive PSF and a facing that continues to enjoy strong tenant and buyer demand.

Today, a similar 3-bedroom unit in the same development is transacting at over $250 PSF higher, translating into significant paper gains.

Value Asset Building Through Strategic Entry & Exit

Another client purchased a freehold 2-bedroom, 1-bath unit in Aljunied, with the long-term objective of upgrading to a larger home. At the point of exit, market conditions were challenging: over 30 competing units within the same development, some listings priced below our asking price, and many units unsold for over a year.

By anchoring decisions to a value-driven framework, we positioned the property correctly and achieved the highest gross profit in the entire development.

On the re-entry, we secured a new purchase that was approximately $150 PSF undervalued compared to neighbouring transactions completed in the same month — creating immediate paper gains upon purchase.
