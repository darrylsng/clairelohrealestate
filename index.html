Your MOP is up. You made a profit. You’re thinking, what’s next?

With new launches approaching $3,100 PSF and resale properties trading around $2,200–$2,300 PSF, the question isn’t simply whether you can afford to upgrade. It’s whether you’re making the right move.

The Singapore property market has changed. What worked for previous generations of HDB upgraders may not necessarily work today.

With higher property prices, increased Buyer’s Stamp Duty (BSD), a longer 4-year Seller’s Stamp Duty (SSD) holding period and changing interest rate conditions, the cost of getting your next purchase wrong has become significantly higher.

And if you’re planning to upgrade in 2027, there are a few things you need to consider before committing to your next property.

01 The Price Gap

Between new launch and resale.

The price gap between new launches and resale is getting harder to ignore.

When new launches are approaching $3,100 PSF while comparable resale opportunities may be available at $2,200–$2,300 PSF, we need to ask a more important question:

Are you paying for genuine value, or simply paying a premium for something new?

A new launch isn’t automatically a better investment.
Neither is a cheaper resale property automatically a better buy.

What matters is understanding the entry price, the surrounding competition, future demand and, most importantly, who your next buyer will be when it’s time for you to exit.

02 Cost of Error

Why the margin for error is thinner.

The cost of making a wrong move is much higher today.

Property upgrading used to be a relatively straightforward exercise for many homeowners.
Buy, hold for a few years, sell and move on.

But the equation has changed.

  • Higher property prices mean a larger capital commitment.
  • Higher BSD increases your upfront acquisition costs.
  • The 4-year SSD holding period means you need to plan your exit much more carefully, depending on your age.
  • Interest rate movements can affect your monthly cash flow and holding costs.
  • Higher entry prices leave less room for mistakes.

So the questions today revolve around:

Can I afford it if rates go up? Who is my next buyer? What’s my exit at year 5 and year 10? What if my HDB sells for less than expected?

Today, your exit strategy should be considered before you even enter the property.

03 HDB Supply

Why tomorrow’s buyer matters.

Future HDB supply could change the resale landscape.

HDB continues to plan for a substantial pipeline of new flats over the longer term.

While this doesn’t mean HDB prices will necessarily decline, future supply and competition are important factors to consider.

If you’re upgrading from an HDB flat, you need to think beyond just buying a condo — but understanding the demand & supply factors would directly impact your exit.

It’s not just about buying a property that you like today. It’s about buying one that someone else will want tomorrow.

So, what should you be focusing on?

01 Focus area

Your objectives

Why are you moving?

Think about the school run, living closer to family or sharing a home with your parents. Do you need more space now, or a home that will suit you in retirement?

02 Focus area

Capital protection

You’ve spent at least five years building equity in your HDB. How much of it are you comfortable putting into your next home?

Look at the entry price, holding costs and competing supply. If you need to sell in a weaker market, who is likely to buy?

A popular launch can still carry more risk than you want to take.

03 Focus area

A better financial future

What do you want this move to make possible?

You might want to upgrade again, build a property portfolio, fund your children’s education or retire with fewer financial commitments.

Work out how much room the purchase leaves for those plans.

04 Most important

A comfortable transition

  1. Time the sale and purchaseIf an ABSD refund applies, check the deadline. Where will you live between homes?
  2. Work out the cash proceedsAfter repaying your loan and refunding CPF savings plus accrued interest, how much cash will you have?
  3. Check your borrowing capacityCheck the Total Debt Servicing Ratio (TDSR) and loan stress test. What monthly payment feels comfortable?
  4. Plan for a longer searchIf you sell your HDB first, what will you do if the right condo takes longer to find?

Strategic Upgrade Within the Same District (D15)

In 2016, I helped a client purchase a 2-bedroom unit in District 15. As his family plans evolved and the need to upgrade became clearer, we identified the right next move — one that aligned with his budget without over-leveraging, and prioritised proximity to good schools.

We timed the sale decisively and sold it without viewing, achieving $300,000 in profit over 3 years. The proceeds were reinvested into a 3-bedroom + study unit within the same district, secured at an attractive PSF and a facing that continues to enjoy strong tenant and buyer demand.

Aljunied · freehold 2-bedroom

Value Asset Building Through Strategic Entry & Exit

Another client purchased a freehold 2-bedroom, 1-bath unit in Aljunied, with the long-term objective of upgrading to a larger home. At the point of exit, market conditions were challenging: over 30 competing units within the same development, some listings priced below our asking price, and many units unsold for over a year.

By anchoring decisions to a value-driven framework, we positioned the property correctly and achieved the highest gross profit in the entire development.

On the re-entry, we secured a new purchase that was approximately $150 PSF undervalued compared to neighbouring transactions completed in the same month — creating immediate paper gains upon purchase.
